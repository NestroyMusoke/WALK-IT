## 3 November: the folded two-panel start target, and the inertial scale question

The user's instruction was to find the loophole rather than run another recording session that would fail, and to work out whether the metric scale that Apple Measure and ARKit get from an accelerometer is reachable from a browser. Both were answered with experiments before any product code was touched.

**The diagnosis was structural, not a tuning problem.** A single 0.16 m planar square is being asked to supply orientation, metric scale and a unique pose at once. IPPE returns two solutions for a plane; `MARKER_POSE_MIN_SECOND_ERROR_RATIO: 1.5` correctly refuses the bad ones; what survives is a depth along the camera axis that corner refinement cannot fix — measured earlier at 3.83 cm versus 3.81 cm, because `dZ/du = Z/u` is 3.94 cm per corner pixel at 1.25 m. OpenCV issue #8813 states the cure: *"If you have one or more additional markers that are not co-planar to the original marker, then you can resolve the ambiguity."* arXiv 2509.17345v2 independently reports lower positioning error for 90° marker arrangements.

**The implementation.** `geometry/marker_anchor.py` gains `folded_panel_corners`, `noncoplanarity_rank_ratio`, `detect_marker_panels` and `estimate_folded_target_pose`, and `estimate_start_marker_pose` now tries the joint path only when `MARKER_PANEL_COUNT > 1` **and** more than one configured panel is actually detected. `detect_start_marker` and `estimate_marker_pose` were left byte-identical so the single-square path and its tests are untouched. Five config keys were added: `MARKER_PANEL_COUNT: 2`, `MARKER_COMPANION_ID: 1`, `MARKER_FOLD_ANGLE_DEG: 90`, `MARKER_PANEL_QUIET_MARGIN_M: 0.025`, `MARKER_MIN_NONCOPLANARITY_RANK_RATIO: 0.05`. `geometry/marker_scan.py` forwards `panelCount` and `nonCoplanarityRankRatio` into the receipt, and both were added to the numeric-only telemetry allowlist in `server/marker-scan-handler.ts`. `geometry/marker_print.py` now emits one sheet per panel with `--panel`, plus the fold and tape instructions, and regenerates all four sheets (`design/` and `apps/web/public/`) when run without it.

**A finding that changed the print.** The first rendered fold was detected 0/25. Direct probing showed the cause: `cv2.aruco.generateImageMarker` tiles are not detected standalone, and two panels touching at the fold merge into one blob ArUco cannot segment. The quiet zone is therefore a physical print requirement, encoded as `MARKER_PANEL_QUIET_MARGIN_M: 0.025`, and `geometry/test_marker_print.py` pins it to the A4 layout's own border — `(210 − 160) / 2 = 25 mm` — so paper and geometry cannot drift apart. `test_panels_touching_at_the_fold_lose_the_fold` asserts the failure rather than assuming the fix.

**The inertial scale question, answered and then deliberately deprioritised.** Browsers do expose an accelerometer: `window.DeviceMotionEvent` gives `accelerationIncludingGravity` in m/s² and `rotationRate` in deg/s. A zero-velocity-updated double integration — subtract the segment mean, enforce v(0)=v(T)=0 — cancels any constant error vector, which covers both sensor bias and gravity leaked by a constant attitude error, the dominant term. Simulated across 400 trials per cell over 0.30–0.80 m, 1.0–2.5 s and 60/30 Hz: 0.50 m in 1.5 s at 60 Hz gives 1.7% median scale error, p90 4.1%, 96% within 5% and 100% within 10%; 0.80 m in 1.0 s gives 0.4% median and 100% within 5%; aggregated over a 6 s window the median falls from 1.7% (one segment) to 0.9% (four). Idle cross-axis residual is 0.94 cm median, 2.08 cm p90. So it can supply scale, and it can corroborate the marker at roughly the 30% level. It was **not** wired into the product, because the folded target already supplies metric scale from a known physical edge length, and shipping an untested IMU path hours before a field session would add risk without adding a capability the demo needs. Stated risks if it is picked up later: exact standstill at both segment ends, a known motion axis, detected segment boundaries, and on iOS Safari a user gesture plus `DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission()`. Any such cross-check must be additive and strictly failing, and must report honest absence when permission is denied.
